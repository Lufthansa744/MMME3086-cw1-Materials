Ansys workbench is a comprehensively large piece of software with many attributes.  We only use

Desktop, save the project to your own OneDrive.  Or if you are using a desktop or laptop you can

6. Coursework preparation Section - Making an aerofoil in a duct – 
like a small wind tunnel 
Next make it a bit more complex still by inserting an airfoil.  The equation of the NACA0012 airfoil is: 
𝑦𝑡 =5𝑡ℎ[0.2969√𝑥−0.1260𝑥−0.3516𝑥2+0.2843𝑥3−0.1015𝑥4] 
Where th is the thickness ratio from the NACA serial code (e.g. NACA0012 has 12% of chord as 
thickness, so th=0.12), so put it as the equation, and it is required in parametric form using the 
parametric coordinate [u], which is the x-coordinate in Spaceclaim: 
x = 100t 
y = 500*0.12*(0.2969*Sqrt(t)-0.126*t-0.3516*t*t+0.2843*t*t*t-0.1015*t*t*t*t)

7. How to make an aerofoil in the duct on Discovery 
Use the Equation tool in the Design ribbon and select the Custom type from the end of the drop-
down list of types and select ‘Curve’ on the equation box that opens (use the double arrow if it isn’t 
visible to make it drop down).   
 
Figure 13 Design ribbon with capital Sigma for the Equation developer 
Enter the formula in the appropriate y direction for the thickness of the aerofoil and put x as 100t 
and the formula for y above, which are the parametric variables.  Change the U end from 6.28 to 1.  
Use a scale multiplier of 500 on y and 100 on x to make the aerofoil sufficiently big.  Press the tick in 
the view port to confirm this side of the aerofoil. 
 
Figure 14 The equation editor and the plotted aerofoil surface 
Do the same again with a negative sign on the y direction formula to make the other side of the 
aerofoil.  You will see that the two curves have a y direction length distance between the trailing 
edges.  Use the sketch tool to join the points at the end of the aerofoil where there is a gap, on the 
side where the main duct surface is, i.e. at the end where the axes indicator (x, y, z) is.  Make sure 
you have the right sketch plane selected by clicking on it (click on the face-normal direction) when 
you select the sketch tool, i.e. for the x-y plane, click on the z axis indicator arrow.  End sketch.   
Select the edges of the top aerofoil surface on the x-y plane, the bottom surface edge and the line 
that connects the two ends, using ctrl to select multiple.  Select Fill from the Design ribbon and you 
will see a new surface appears, the aerofoil shape.  Now Select and delete the upper and lower 
curves and the end line that defined the aerofoil. 
You can rotate the aerofoil to a chosen angle of attack: right click on the duct and ‘hide’ it, so you 
can see the aerofoil clearly (or click on the eye in the drop down at the left ‘Design1’. Activate the 3D 
mode (Figure 13), then select the aerofoil shape and click on ‘Move’; select the angle changing 
curved arrow and drag slightly before entering your angle to fix its rotation, say, 8 . (or maybe 352 
if that provides a positive angle for y direction).  Right click in the screen and ‘show all’.
