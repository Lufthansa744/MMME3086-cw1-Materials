| Context
The learning outcome(s) of the coursework assessment is to develop a 2D CFD model that represents the 
flow around an aerofoil in a duct. This will help you to develop aspects of all the learning outcomes of the 
module. 
The CFD assessment provides an opportunity to learn how to use a commercially available CFD software 
package. You will set up the model, solve the fluid flow and analyse the data produced. The procedure will 
involve establishing that a proper CFD investigation has been done including mesh dependence check, 
numerical convergence and analysis of physical representation according to validation data available in the 
literature.

Task (A) – generating a geometry and mesh [16% of coursework 
marks] 
The purpose of this task is to gain some hands-on experience of setting up a geometry using Ansys Discovery 
and various computational meshes in Ansys Mesher. 
Using the step-by-step consolidation method (available on Echo360) 
A1. Generate your own 150 mm by 1 m duct with a NACA0012 aerofoil at an 8 angle of attack. Illustrate the 
geometry with a short descriptive caption in Figure 1.        [4] 
 
A2. Generate and illustrate in Figure 2  a mesh with 10 mm triangular cells on the duct, but with 2 mm cell 
Sizing associated with the walls of the aerofoil only and a growth rate of 1.05 of the cells away from the 
aerofoil.  In the caption (<100 words) add a comment on your consideration of the model about the CFD 
theory of the mesh. Display the local region around the aerofoil in an inset image.  
[8] 
A3. Add appropriate names to all of the boundaries of the model and label them in your figure from part A2.

Task (B) – generating boundary conditions and solving 
[34% of coursework marks] 
Set up a case using the aerofoil mesh you have generated .  Set the velocity inlet magnitude to the value 
(n/100)10+50 m/s, where n is the number formed by the last two digits of your student ID number (should 
produce Re = O(10 5) based on chord length of 100 mm) and use the default pressure outlet boundary 
condition.  On models, select under viscous model the settings for each of the 5 cases described in sections 
B1 and B2 below. 
B0. Note in your report            [6]: 
a. density of air 
b. viscosity of air 
c. turbulence intensity and isotropic RMS velocity of turbulent fluctuations at inlet 
d. velocity at inlet 
e. calculated Reynolds number based on chord length 
B1. Case 1 : Use the Spalart-Allmaras one equation turbulence mode l using standard wall function  
together with the SIMPLE pressure-velocity coupling method. Initialise the case and set the calculation  
and run it until convergence is achieved .  Print a graphic, in Figure 3, showing the residual history and 
note in the caption what happened to the convergence of the case and what you think it might indicate.  
              [5] 
 
Note the number of iterations to convergence. From the data , extract to an Excel spreadsheet the 
following:  
1. static pressure and y+ on suction side,  
2. static pressure and y+ on pressure side.  
3. Insert three lines, across the duct, one at mid chord, one at trailing edge and one 100 mm 
downstream from the trailing edge. Extract to Excel the velocity magnitude for the three lines.  
4. Record the total drag and lift on the aerofoil  and the convergence criterion and iterations to 
convergence.  
5. Record this data for all subsequent models also. 
 
Case 2: Set up a new case with the same mesh by the ‘Duplicate’ method in Workbench. Run it with k- 
turbulence model, standard wall function and SIMPLE pressure-velocity coupling and record the data 
for this model. 
Case 3: Duplicate the setup  again, and run the RSM model, with standard wall function , and with 
SIMPLE pressure-velocity coupling method. Record the data. 
 
B2. Results analysis: For the RSM Case 3, present a vector plot around the aerofoil showing the wake 
region clearly in Figure 4.  In the caption state what it is you are showing and what it is that you see in 
the flow pattern and state the lift and drag for this case. State the lift and drag forces and the iterations to 
convergence and the convergence criterion.         [6]  
In Figure 5, present a contour plot of y+ values on the cells in touch with the aerofoil for Case 3, showing 
the cell values and showing the legend to indicate range of values of the cells in contact with the aerofoil. 
Indicate any unusual regions stating why you have highlighted them with reference to the wall function 
that was applied.             [5]  
 
B3. Load the mesh Aerofoil8-degAoA-enhancedBL.msh into Fluent directly. Run the Case 4 with RSM and 
enhanced wall treatment with SIMPLE, state what you observe. Run the Case 4 again with SIMPLE, 
k- turbulence model, and use enhanced wall treatment in the turbulence model. Note the convergence 
rate and the drag and lift forces and record its data as for the models in B1. Duplicate and set up a new 
Case 5 to change the turbulence method back to RSM, alter the under-relaxation on Reynolds stresses 
to 0.25, all other turbulent and momentum under-relaxation parameters to 0.5, and then continue to run 
the case until convergence or until it doesn’t change. Collect the data. Report the drag and lift forces and 
describe how the residuals behave for each of Case 4 and Case 5 and comment what changed between 
the two RSM cases here.            [6].  
Present a graphic in Figure 6 containing both a graphic showing the velocity vectors and another graphic 
of the y+ for the RSM case and describe in the caption what you can see and interpret the outcome with 
respect to CFD theory.           [6]
