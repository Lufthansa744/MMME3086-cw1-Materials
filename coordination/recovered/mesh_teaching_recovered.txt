1. Hurdle 1: How to access Ansys Workbench 
Ansys workbench is a comprehensively large piece of software with many attributes.  We only use 
SpaceClaim, Mesher and Fluent. 
How to download: 
Click on the Ansys Student icon on the website link below.  Download the zip file.  Extract and on the 
extracted files click on the setup.exe file. 
You can either download Ansys Workbench 2026 R1 (takes about 15 mins to download and 20 mins 
to install)  
Figure 1 the icon used on the Ansys download link 
Or go to one of the engineering desktops in the faculty or register to use the virtual engineering 
desktop using the Windows App on your own computer if you choose to work on your own 
machine. 
If the screen resolution is unusually small, then you can try the following: right click on the 
Workbench start icon/item in task menu and select ‘open file location’; locate the Workbench start 
exe file and right click and click on ‘properties’ and on the ‘compatibility’ tab select ‘change high DPI 
settings’ and select the ‘override high DPI….’ And try system enhanced or system. 
2. Hurdle 1a: where to save to. 
Open Ansys workbench from All Programs.  Before you begin, if you are using the virtual Engineering 
Desktop, save the project to your own OneDrive.  Or if you are using a desktop or laptop you can 
save to the desktop or to your OneDrive, which should appear on the list of files on the file explorer.  
If Workbench crashes at any time on the virtual desktop, type ‘task manager’ into the task bar and 
close the program in the usual way for crashed programs. 
And one thing further before you begin, whenever it looks like Workbench is doing nothing and you 
don’t know what is happening, look at the bottom left of the Workbench window – it says what it is 
doing at that time.  The virtual desktop can take unusually long (order of 5 minutes) at times for 
some reason.

4. Mesh generation 
Now turn attention to the computational mesh.  Double click on Mesh and your geometry will 
appear in a new ‘mesher’ window.  It takes about a minute to open fully, when you will see the 
geometry appear. 
 
Figure 6 the model tree shows Geometry is completed with a tick

Select edge selection tool (Figure 7) and select one of the edges with left click, then right click and 
select ‘create named selection’ from the list that appears, and choose an appropriate name for that 
boundary – this will become an item for setting a boundary condition on.  Select each bounding edge 
in turn giving it a boundary name: in this case opposing short edges are ‘inlet’ and ‘outlet’, and 
opposing long edges are ‘wall1’ and ‘wall2’.  Click on named selections in the panel on the left to see 
them. 
 
Figure 8 project panel on the left of screen, where Named Selections can be clicked on and seen on the model, and where 
the Mesh creator can be selected, and the Details of “Mesh” panel that appears below when Mesh is selected 
Click on Mesh in the outline panel on the left, and you will see the mesh details panel below (Figure 
8).  Right click on Mesh and click on Generate Mesh – there will be a notice at the bottom left of the 
GUI that it is meshing.  Notice that the mesh is very coarse after generation.  In order to have a flow 
represented by a CFD model there must be on the smallest flow cross-section at least 5 volume cells 
across in order to allow for zero slip at the wall and a flow profile to be represented. 
There are various options under in the Details of “Mesh” panel for controlling how refined or dense 
the mesh is.  Select ‘Element Size’ on the ‘Defaults’ section (Figure 8) and type in a new size of cell 
edge length defined in the units shown in the box to the right (try 10 mm, i.e. 0.01 m – you should 
see the unit to the right of the number you have typed in) and see what happens to the mesh (to 
regenerate and see the mesh, right click on the mesh in the model tree and ‘clear generated data’ 
and then again ‘generate mesh’ or just ‘update mesh’).  To see the mesh again, select the geometry

Now we turn attention to controlling the mesh.  Although automatic mesh generation is quick and 
easy, it isn’t usually best. 
There are quadrilateral cells and triangle cells, or a combination of them.  Right click ‘Mesh’ in the 
panel on the left and Insert and Method; click in ‘Geometry’ (Figure 10 at the top) and right click on 
the model geometry in the window using the ‘face’ selection tool , and click 
‘Apply’; select Method and choose Triangles.  Now right click on Mesh and select ‘clear data’ and  
‘generate mesh’.  The mesh regenerates with triangular elements – left click on ‘mesh’ and if still not 
visible then somewhere in the view screen to see it.

Now we experiment with the node distribution on the edges.  This is quite tricky, it’s important to 
get the steps right. 
Right click on ‘Mesh’ in the left panel to adjust the mesh you just created and ‘clear generated data’.  
And delete the triangle method by right click on it and delete in the panel under Mesh.  Then to 
refine an edge select the edge selection tool (Figure 7 again) and: 
1. left click on one of the long edges of the model and it should turn green,  
2. right click it and then left click on Insert and Sizing and an edge length control will appear in 
the panel on the left,  
3. It should say edge sizing on the menu item which appears on the left panel underneath 
‘Mesh’ 
4. Left click on the edge sizing item and the details appear on the panel below it on which you 
can select an edge cell element size,  
‘Type’ allows you to choose an option about how the scale of the cells will be set (e.g. by 
element size, number of divisions on the edge, sphere of influence) and see what happens when 
you generate the mesh with a 5 mm element size.  Note that the element size must be smaller 
than the global cell size already selected on the surface method previously (Figure 8).  You can 
have edge sizing selections for each of your edges.  Apply 5 mm on the long wall edges and see 
the effect  Update Mesh to see the result and to translate to the setup for Fluent (the next 
step).  The mesh structure is a bit of a mess, and generally a more smoothly transitioning mesh 
structure would be desirable to improve the solution accuracy.  Try it again with the triangular 
cell method.  And finally try it with a Growth Rate of 1.05 on the edge sizing setting pane.  You 
will see that you have some control over the appearance of the mesh.   
The Update step on the Mesh (right click on Mesh and select Update) is important because it 
translates the mesh to Fluent. 
Close Mesh and you will see that the Mesh has now a tick to show it is successfully done. 
Lastly you can update the mesh to ensure that it exports to Fluent, if you didn’t already, from the 
Workbench panel by right clicking on the Mesh and selecting Update.  A green tick should be by the 
side of the Mesh on the Workbench panel when it is successful.
